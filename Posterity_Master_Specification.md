#  **WHAT IS POSTERITY**

### **Legacy System**

Posterity is a post-life legacy app that allows users to create custom content and schedule its delivery across the years and milestones that matter most. The app follows this schedule to deliver the content — which includes messages, photos, and videos — via social media, text messaging, or email after our customers have passed on. Customers leave behind their own words, in their own voice — and it arrives when, how, and where they decide. 

### **Posterity Social**

Alongside the legacy product is Posterity Social — a built-in social network for older adults living with late-life issues such as aging, illness, or mortality. Nothing shared or communicated within Posterity Social can be exported from or captured within the app. This walled system cultivates a safe space for discourse on the difficult subjects in the lives of our elders, and will create a culture of honest and productive dialogue within the network. Users find one another through a search system that, rather than appearance or popularity, prioritizes personal growth. Through the network's built-in systems — direct messaging, global chat, posts, and profiles — users meet others in similar life circumstances. These relationships will help fine-tune our customers' legacy content and, more importantly, add to the health and happiness of their late-life transition. Posterity Social is a collaborative, walled space where honest communication is expressed without the fear of judgement or difference that the elderly so often experience. It is a social network intended to bring joy rather than apprehension — not just another feed to perform on, but a place our users can enjoy and learn from. 

# **ACCOUNT LIFECYCLE & PHASES**

Posterity accounts move through six distinct phases, in sequence. It is important to understand that only accounts shift phases. Plans exist within accounts and move alongside them— plans do not independently progress between phases. Think of the account as the container and the plans as its contents. When the account moves forward, its contents move with it.

This language appears throughout the product, the onboarding walkthrough, and all customer-facing materials. It is a core part of what makes Posterity unique \- clinical terminology is removed entirely. The system responds only to events and dates, never to the customer's passing itself.

### **Key Terms**

* **Legacy** \- The complete body of scheduled content contained within a Posterity account. Includes all messages, videos, photos, and notes currently created by the user.  A second legacy can be created alongside the original in which the customer decides how the specific post, or the legacy as a whole, will live on after its completion.

* **Silence** — The confirmed condition that a customer is no longer speaking for themselves. Silence is established rather than assumed.  When Posterity contact attempts to a customer go unanswered, after a pattern of failed check-ins have been observed, a state of Silence is confirmed within the system.  If a customer pre-authorizes it within their account, a designated user can also contact Posterity to confirm Silence.  

* **Account Trigger** — The event that moves an account out of its building period and into delivery. It fires automatically once Silence has been confirmed, and can be activated manually by a customer at any time using the **Begin** option within their profile.  The trigger can also fire when a customer does not respond to a Legacy Guard's confirmation, as described within the Legacy Guard System section. The trigger fires only on an initiated account, whichever route activates it.

* **Initiate** \- To pay for a plan. This is the first non-storage fee payment within the system. Approval of a Grace plan request initiates an account in the same way. An initiated account has been at least partially funded by paying for some or all of the plans within it. Initiation commits Posterity to retaining and storing the funded content within the system.

* **Delivery** \- The successful transfer of content to its intended recipient and/or recipients, defined by outcome rather than by transmission method. A piece of content has been delivered when it reaches the person it was written for, by whatever method carried it there. If a preferred method cannot be completed as intended, Posterity pursues every remaining path to that same recipient by reattempting transmission and routing through alternative configured channels. 

### **The Six Phases**

*Phase 1*:  **Horizon** \- The customer has subscribed to Horizon at $9.99/year.  Within this phase, users can begin building their legacy prior to any real financial commitment.

*Phase 2*:  **Planning** \- The customer has initiated their account by paying for at least one plan. Content continues to be refined, and they begin checking in with Posterity every six months. This is typically the longest phase, potentially spanning years or decades.  It lasts from the moment of initiation to the firing of the Account Trigger.

*Phase 3*: **Interlude** \- A trigger event has occurred, and the account enters a quiet pause.  No activity can exist within this pause.  The Interlude Phase lasts from the trigger event to January 1st of the first active plan year. Where no years are skipped prior to the first plan, this is a maximum of just under 12 months. Where years are skipped prior to the first plan, Interlude extends through each of them. The pause in account flow during Interlude allows the bereaved time to process their loss prior to receiving their loved ones content.  Additionally, it allows the calendar to adapt its post schedules into a fresh year without the offset caused by leap years.

*Phase 4*: **Active** \- The account is delivering content according to the customer's calendar. Activation occurs automatically on calendar day 1 of the first year following the account entering the Interlude phase, after any initial years the customer skips. The system automatically generates that year's delivery schedule.  This phase repeats annually for each plan year that has been initiated within an account. Skipped years between plans will require a non-recurring storage fee. This fee will be automatically added to the checkout cart every time a year is skipped over during plan selection. Storage fees stack per skipped year, and are paid along with their corresponding plan upon its Initiation.  

*Phase 5*: **Reprise** \- The account's final scheduled post has been delivered.  The customer's second legacy opens within the Legacy Guard portal to every Legacy Guard on the account, styled as a photo album. Only content the customer marked to "Include in Posterity" appears there, locked in its latest state.  The Reprise Phase begins on day one of the year following an account's final active plan year, and lasts until the account enters the Posterity Phase. The first year of Reprise carries no storage fee. Additional years can be added at any point through the purchase of non-recurring storage fees.  These can be paid in advance by the customer, or added by a Legacy Guard from within their portal. 

###### The Legacy Guard Sequence

When an account's final scheduled post is confirmed delivered, the Communication Dispatch System sends a message to the account's primary Legacy Guard. The message informs the Legacy Guard that the customer's legacy is complete.  It then encourages them to log into the Legacy Guard portal to see what role, if any, they will play in the customer's second legacy. If the primary Legacy Guard does not respond to the initial message, every remaining Legacy Guard on the account is contacted in all further attempts. Contact attempts repeat once every three months, and end after the fourth attempt where no Legacy Guard has responded or logged into their portal.   A  response from any Legacy Guard, or a Legacy Guard logging into their portal also ends the sequence. Where no response is received, the sequence ends when the account is deleted. Legacy Guards are informed in their onboarding email that the Reprise year can be used to download and share the customer's second legacy content, and that additional years of storage can be purchased to extend the phase for as long as they choose.

###### The Facebook Page Sequences

At the start of each Reprise year, two automatic system checks determine what happens to the customer's Facebook Page. If the customer elected to delete the Page after all account deliveries are finished, a staff task is created to see to its deletion. Where the customer elected to delete the Page with the account, no task is created here and the Page ends at the Posterity Transition instead.  Otherwise, the system checks whether a non-recurring storage fee has been paid for the year ahead. When a storage fee is found, nothing further occurs and the check repeats at the start of the following Reprise year. When no storage fee is detected, a transfer cascade begins.  The Page's co-administrators are then contacted in priority order until one accepts ownership. A storage fee paid during the phase stops the cascade, and a Page still unaccepted at the close of the cascade is deleted. The full sequence is detailed in the Social Media Integration document.

###### The Posterity Transition

At the end of each Reprise year, the system checks whether a storage fee has been paid for the year ahead. Where one exists, the account remains in Reprise and the check simply repeats at the end of the following year. This cycle continues for as long as fees are paid. Where none is found, the account is tabled for deletion, and an Operational Dashboard task is generated directing staff to confirm the absence of payment and delete the account. Where the customer elected to delete the Page with the account, the same task directs staff to delete the Page. Following its deletion, the account takes the final step in its cycle and enters the Posterity Phase.  

*Phase 6*: **Posterity**  \- Users embark on the journey through Posterity to build a bridge across generations. In plain terms, they use our legacy platform to speak directly to their descendants after they are gone. We provide the means for people to build a living legacy. Every user is drawn to Posterity by a universal human desire: to be remembered by those they leave behind. While our product is difficult to quantify, at our core, we are a content-generation engine with an expansive delivery window—one designed to reach into a future beyond our own time in this world. Our customers know the best way to reach the people they love. It is that love, expressed through meaningful end-of-life communication, that fuels the timeless content we preserve.

The **Posterity Phase** is where a legacy lives on once it has reached its audience. It is the love and memories shared in life, reaching forward into tomorrow. The content created in Posterity represents the final time our users will speak to their loved ones, and we expect raw honesty and emotional openness. It is our hope that the content will be as profound an experience to create as it is to receive—deepening its audience's appreciation for the life and love they shared with our customers, and often forging new memories to go alongside the old. These living memories are where every Posterity account ultimately rests. They define the Posterity Phase, and every system within our platform aligns sequentially to serve as the engine that brings them to life.

# **PLAN YEAR SYSTEM**

* Each plan covers one year of content delivery. A customer's account contains one or more plans, each assigned a tier (Basic, Premium, Legacy, Custom, or Grace) and a plan year (Year 1, Year 2, Year 3, etc.). Tiers can vary plan to plan \- Year 1 might be Premium, Year 2 might be Legacy, Year 3 might be skipped (storage fee applies), Year 4 might be Basic. Plan years count from account activation forward, not from subscription date or calendar year.

* Unless the customer chooses to skip years prior to the start of their first plan, year 1 begins January 1 of the first calendar year following the account entering Interlude.

* Payment timeline and delivery timeline tracked separately. 

* Outside of any grace or warning periods, payment of storage fees is necessary in order to avoid account deletion.

* Skipped plan years (years for which no plan is built) generate non-recurring storage fees automatically added to the customer's checkout cart, stacking per skipped year.

* Customers can pay for multiple years upfront.

* Plans are built while an account's storage fee is current, and can be paid for individually or together from within the cart. Paying for the first plan initiates the account and moves it into the Planning Phase, though unpaid plans can continue to be built alongside those already funded. Grace and Custom plans are the exception, under the rule held within Custom Plans and Posterity Grace.

* Content calendar in User Profiles, built around real calendar dates tied to plan years.

* Funds for Year 2 not accessed until Year 1 is complete.

* Year-by-year fund release automated via server-side cronjob.

\-Allows users access to Posterity's built in social media network.

**STORAGE LAPSE GRACE PERIOD**

When a storage payment lapses, the following automated notification sequence fires. It applies to Horizon and Planning Phase accounts, and to accounts whose three-month free trial has expired. A lapsed payment and an expired trial each carry their own unique first and final notification. It never reaches an account in Interlude or any later phase.

* For a lapsed payment, the sequence begins once Stripe's automatic charge fails its single retry, three days after the first attempt, or once a payment request goes unpaid past its due date. It runs for one year.

* First notification: unique message explaining the lapsed payment or expired trial, account status, and next steps, distinct from the standard reminder sequence.

* Months 1 through 6: one notification every other month. Months 7 through 10: one notification each month. Month 11: two notifications. Month 12: one notification each week. Each is labeled with its sequence position, for example "Reminder 3 of 13."

* Final notification: unique message confirming the account has been deleted, distinct from the standard reminder sequence.

* For Planning Phase accounts, notifications state that funded plans are deleted along with the account

* Legacy Guards holding payment authority receive every reminder from month 10 onward.

* During the year, the customer can log in and view all content. Building and editing continue for the first three months and then lock until storage is current. Every notification sent before the lock states the date it takes effect. For Planning Phase accounts, the Check-in System continues, and the Begin button and Legacy Guard confirmation remain available.

* The sequence ends when storage is paid or when the Account Trigger fires. A payment within the year begins a new storage year from the date of payment.

* No payment within the year: the account is deleted.

* Notifications are sent through the Communication Dispatch System and follow its channel policy.

# **CHECK-IN SYSTEM (PLANNING PHASE)**

* Customer checks in every 6 months via email, SMS, or a check-in button within User Profiles. Each check-in confirmation reminds the customer to review the contact details within their account.

* Missed check-in triggers the following automated notification sequence:

\-First notification: unique message explaining the missed check-in and next steps \- distinct from the standard reminder sequence.

\-Two notifications sent to the customer each month for a total of 3 months. Each notification is labeled with its sequence position (e.g. "Reminder 2 of 6").

\-Notifications sent to the customer and to Legacy Guards through the Communication Dispatch System, following its channel policy and the primary-first rule stated in the Legacy Guard System section.

\-Final notification: unique message confirming the Account Trigger has fired- distinct from the standard reminder sequence.

* All notifications fully automated \- first and last are unique in content, middle notifications are standard automated reminders with sequence labels.

* No response after all 6 notifications: Account Trigger automatically generated, account moves into Interlude Phase.

* This applies to Planning Phase accounts only (separate from the storage lapse system).

# **LEGACY GUARD SYSTEM**

**Legacy Guard** \- person, or persons, a customer names to act on their behalf when they no longer can. Where the Master Recipient receives content, a Legacy Guard holds authority — confirming an account should move forward, accepting ownership of the customer's social presence, and, where granted, paying fees.

* Multiple Legacy Guards optional but strongly encouraged \- especially for accounts spanning significant timeframes.

* Within the check-in cascade, only the primary Legacy Guard receives notifications during the first month. Where the primary Legacy Guard does not respond, all remaining notifications go to every Legacy Guard on the account. A response is selecting the acknowledgment link within a notification, or logging into the Legacy Guard portal. A response keeps later notifications with the primary Legacy Guard and does not check the customer in.

* System automatically sends an invitation to create an account within the Legacy Guard portal

* First invitation: unique message explaining their role, what Posterity is, and their next steps \- distinct from the standard resend sequence.

* Invitation automatically resent every 6 months until accepted, each resend sequence-labeled. Can also be resent manually via the Operational Dashboard, or by the customer within Legacy Guard setup.  The invitation carries a decline link. Declining ends the resend sequence automatically, and the customer is notified so another Legacy Guard can be named.

* Customer receives notifications alongside Legacy Guards reminding them that a Legacy Guard has not yet set up their account.

* Customer receives notification when a Legacy Guard accepts invitation and sets up their account

* If no Legacy Guard ever sets up their account, the check in, Silence, and Account Trigger systems still functions independently.

* Legacy Guard portal is a separate login on the Posterity site, completely independent from customer account

* Legacy Guard portal shows: their role, linked customer, the customer's personal note, notification history, confirmation button, and a text message switch first offered when the invitation is accepted. During the Reprise Phase it also holds the customer's second legacy and the option to purchase storage years. From Interlude onward it also shows the account's purchase record, with each plan year marked as upcoming, delivering, or complete.

* When Legacy Guard believes customer has passed: they log into their portal and submit confirmation

* Confirmation requires double verification (re-enter password \+ confirmation code sent to their email/phone) to prevent accidental triggering.

* Once confirmed, the customer is notified and given one month to respond. Any check-in from the customer within that month cancels the confirmation. Where the month passes with no check-in, the Account Trigger fires and the account moves into Interlude.

* Customer notifications during that month: a unique first notification when the confirmation is submitted, a sequence-labeled reminder each week, and a unique final notification confirming the Account Trigger has fired. The confirming Legacy Guard is notified if the customer checks in.

* Personal note from customer to Legacy Guard with delivery instructions \- written within Legacy Guard setup in User Profiles, one for each Legacy Guard. The note travels with the invitation and every resend in its current form, and an invitation sent before the note is written goes without it. Once the invitation is accepted, the Legacy Guard portal holds its current version.

* Payment authority \- granted per Legacy Guard by the customer during setup and off by default. When enabled, the Legacy Guard has full authority to make payments on the account's behalf. 

* During the Reprise Phase, Legacy Guards are contacted about their role in the customer's second legacy. The primary Legacy Guard is contacted first, and the remaining Legacy Guards follow where no response is received. The sequence is detailed under Phase 5 in Account Lifecycle and Phases.

* Legacy Guard will need an integrated way to pay for plans within the Legacy Guard portal (tabled for later discussion (See Tabled & Shelved for full scope including plan-funding authority for unfinished accounts and a second trigger type for Horizon-phase confirmation).

* Legacy Guard page added to global nav dropdown for easy portal access.

Recommended onboarding message: "Multiple Legacy Guards are recommended, and strongly encouraged for accounts that plan to span significant timeframes. When an account reaches the end of its Reprise Phase, multiple Legacy Guards increase the likelihood that someone is there and still has the same contact information. Remember, your account might not reach the endpoint on the journey you're about to create for it for years, or even decades. It is also recommended to have Legacy Guards that span age ranges that fit your vision for the life of your account."

# **CONTENT CREATION & DELIVERY** 

### **The Content Builder**

The **Content Builder** is where a customer's legacy is made. Every piece of content in an account is created here — its file, its title, the plan year it belongs to, the date it arrives, and the people it reaches. A piece of content is assembled one part at a time, and it remains a draft until the customer chooses to finalize it. Any edit made to a finalized piece of content returns it to a draft state, including the deletion of an entry from its Delivery Path. The content then remains a draft until the customer commits it once more. A piece of content cannot be finalized without a Primary Recipient and a delivery date, since it needs both the person it was written for and the day it arrives. All other delivery requirements are enforced as entries are built, so finalization never needs to check them. A piece of content can be finalized while a delivery entry is still in progress. The unfinished entry is discarded, and the customer is warned before this occurs with the following message: "You have an unfinished delivery entry that will be lost if your content leaves its draft state. Continue to finalization?" Content is fully editable throughout the Horizon and Planning Phases, and locks in its latest state when the account enters Interlude, whether draft or finalized. A piece still missing its Primary Recipient or delivery date at that point is not delivered and remains within the account.   

* Opens on top of the rest of the Posterity application rather than replacing it. It is collapsible, and it does not close the page it was opened from, so a customer can leave it open while working elsewhere within the app.  When minimized, the Content Builder will need to be right aligned in a tab at the bottom of the application.  Note 8/6-  If it is decided that other applications can benefit from a minimize option, Posterity Social comes to mind, a tab bar will need to be added to the application.  

* Rectangular in shape. 

*  File upload (video, photo, GIF, message).

* One photo, GIF, or video allowed per delivery.

* Auto-generated metadata: file type, creation date, post date.

* A title.

* Delivery date \- the real calendar date the piece arrives within its plan year.

* "Include in Posterity" toggle, with delivery instruction input field when turned on. Content marked this way is made available to Legacy Guards for download and sharing during the Reprise Phase, and the delivery instruction carries the customer's wishes for how it should be passed on. The toggle is off by default and has no bearing on the content's original delivery — it governs only what forms the second legacy.

* All metadata stored as Supabase record linked to delivery automation Plan year+ recipient \+ scheduling controls.

* Select a plan \- customer picks the plan before adding content, from the plans already within the account. Only plans that can hold the piece as it stands are offered, with enough remaining allowance for its delivery type and enough Additional Recipient slots for its entries.

* Saved Recipients \- A list of stored recipients kept within each Posterity account, retrievable while configuring a piece of content's deliveries. Each Saved Recipient is a single labelled entry holding an email address and an optional phone number. A section for adding and editing Saved Recipients is accessible within User Profiles.  The list can also be added to when new recipients, if a customer elects to add them, are created within the Delivery Builder (described within the Delivery System document).  The list applies across all plans within an account.   

* Content can be sent to up to six recipients at a time: one Primary Recipient and up to five Additional Recipients. Alternative Recipients fall outside this count, as they are only attempted when a delivery ahead of them has failed. Full detail is held within the Delivery System document. 

* Handwritten note attachment per message.

* Building calendar \- the content calendar as it appears within the Content Builder, showing where the current piece sits within the selected plan year.

* Draft or finalized state displayed clearly on each piece.

* Lock status displayed clearly on each piece during the Horizon and Planning Phases.

* Validation on upload \- auto-reject on size/format/resolution fail with specific error.

* All content is scanned for nudity at upload.  Content carrying a social media entry is additionally checked for potential Meta Community Standards violations when that entry is committed. Detection is advisory until the system has been tested against real content, and becomes blocking once it has. Every social media delivery is reviewed by staff before posting. Both rules are held in full within the Social Media Integration document.

# **THE DELIVERY SYSTEM**

The Delivery System assigns individual pieces of content to their recipients.  Entries are built within a row of cells inside the Content Builder, labeled in system as the Delivery Builder. Each entry sets a recipient type, a delivery method, and a recipient. A committed entry moves to the Delivery Path, where it is read, reordered, edited, or removed. Nothing is built within the path, so it only displays what the builder commits.

Each piece carries one Primary Recipient, set by its first entry. Alternative Recipients stand behind the Primary Recipient and are attempted in priority order upon failed delivery attempts. Additional Recipients receive the piece alongside the Primary Recipient, up to the number of slots the plan containing the piece of content holds. The Master Recipient is the account's route of last resort, and it stands behind every piece.

The builder checks each entry as it is made, therefore, the path never holds any repeated or broken information. Recipient type limits the delivery methods offered, and delivery method limits the recipients who can be reached. Social media is offered as a delivery method only under the Primary Recipient type, and only within an account that has completed social media authorization. The Social Gate is a redundancy system which requires every social media delivery to carry a backup recipient before it is committed, and a confirmed Master Recipient satisfies the gate across the entire account. Where no Master Recipient exists, the social media sequence is locked as  uncommitted while an Alternative Recipient sequence is built beside it. Once that sequence is complete, both commit to the Delivery Path together. Under phone number, the builder offers only Saved Recipients who have agreed to texts.

# **VIDEO & CONTENT DELIVERY**

* Delivered via social media, text message (SMS or MMS via Twilio), and email.

* Facebook Pages is the only social platform in the initial build. Instagram is deferred pending an unresolved question about whether an Instagram Business account inherits the Page's protection from memorialization, and a second Meta application review cycle for its own publishing permission.

* Email/MMS & SMS video delivery via link, not attachment.

* Social media delivery is by public post only. Content routed to social media is published to the customer's Facebook Page and is visible to everyone following it. There is no private or direct-message delivery through any social platform — content intended for a specific person reaches them by email or text. 

* Direct link fallback (video and images) via Twilio SMS if MMS carrier delivery fails. The link is the same either way, and only the carrier path changes. Once every configured recipient for a piece of content has been attempted and logged, the link is sent to the Master Recipient as the route of last resort. The full rule is held within the Delivery System document under Capacity and Delivery Order.

* Secure delivery links are generated via Supabase Storage's built-in signed URL feature — a private, expiring, token-based link to the stored file, requiring no separate service since Supabase Storage already powers the app's file storage.  Links stay active for 90 days, and the video can be downloaded as many times as the recipient wishes throughout this period — each download is a real but currently negligible cost, well within Posterity's existing egress allowance at present scale.

* Delivery link pages default to encouraging download over in-browser playback. 

* Unused video slots can be used as Standard slots (flexible rollover).

* Ability to process multiple posts simultaneously.

* Operationally a video counts as two text message units, reflecting storage, compression, posting complexity, and labour. The weighting appears within the user manual and never on pricing cards.

* Delivery routing \- Each piece of content carries a Primary Recipient and a Primary Delivery Method, and can carry Alternative and Additional Recipients within the limits set out under Capacity and Delivery Order in the Delivery System document. An Alternative Recipient can be the same person reached by another method, so a recipient's own email can stand behind a text to them.

# **VIDEO/IMAGE STORAGE & COMPRESSION**

* Storage via Supabase Storage (low cost at early scale).

* Client-side compression for video via FFmpeg.wasm at point of upload within the Content Builder. 

* Image compression uses compressorjs — a 12KB library wrapping the browser's native Canvas API, versus FFmpeg's 31–32MB WASM bundle. Compression runs only on an image over the per image limit listed below and brings that image within it. A GIF over the limit is rejected, since compression would turn it into a still image. FFmpeg remains the video compression tool; images use a separate, lighter path since loading the full video codec stack to compress a photo isn't warranted. Compressorjs auto-corrects EXIF photo orientation, as in horizontal as opposed to vertical, and guards against oversized image crashes. Note that the tool hasn't been updated in over a year and may be effectively abandoned. If thumbnail quality ever looks soft after resizing, pica is the fallback, trading a larger bundle for sharper downscaling.

### **THE COMMUNICATION DISPATCH SYSTEM**

Every automated message Posterity sends passes through the Communication Dispatch System. It knows what is due, it knows which channel carries it, and it hands the message to that channel. Nothing else within the product sends automatically.

Three channels sit behind it. Email leaves through Postmark, text message leaves through Twilio, and social media publishes to a customer's Facebook Page through Meta. The publishing path for social media is held within the Social Media Integration document. Channel policy is applied at the moment of sending. Email is the universal baseline and is always sent. Text message layers on top of it for any person who has agreed to texts themselves, and no text goes to anyone who has not. One agreement covers every text Posterity sends that person, and it is given against a disclosure of what is sent, how often, and how to stop. Agreement is never a condition of using Posterity. An agreement belongs to the number it was given for, so a number edited within Posterity starts over without one.

Texts go out between eight in the morning and nine at night in the recipient's local time, judged by area code, and a text due outside that window waits for it to open. Email never waits. A text the person has just set off themselves, such as a verification code or the confirmation that follows an agreement, goes immediately. Every agreement is followed by that confirmation, and every text names Posterity, says why the person is receiving it, and says how to stop. Twilio honors a STOP reply automatically, and Posterity records it and moves that person to email. A number not confirmed within the past year is checked against the Federal Communications Commission's Reassigned Numbers Database before a text goes to it, and a match counts as a failed text.

What the system is asked to send enters a queue rather than being sent directly, so a send that fails is retried rather than lost. A send that exhausts its retries is recorded as a failure, and the Operational Dashboard surfaces it as a task.

Every send is recorded by account and by outcome. The record is what allows a delivery to be evidenced years after it happened, which the obligation to deliver on arrival rather than by route depends on.

Message copy is stored as a field the system reads rather than written into the logic that triggers a send. Approved copy therefore replaces placeholder copy as a content change rather than a code change, and a cascade can be built and tested before its copy is written.

Cascades are callers rather than parts of this system. Each cascade's sequence, its cadence, and its recipients are held by the system that owns its trigger. Two kinds of messages sit outside the Communication Dispatch System entirely. Human correspondence through the Posterity email account is written by a person rather than generated. In-app validation messages, meaning what a customer sees when they attempt something the system does not allow, are built within each surface.

# **SOCIAL MEDIA INTEGRATION**

Posterity delivers social media content by holding administrator control of a Facebook Page the customer creates and owns. A Page is a separate platform object from a personal profile, and it survives memorialization of that profile provided another administrator is already in place. # **ADMIN & OPERATIONS** 

## **The Priority System**

The Priority System is a confirmed component of the Operational Dashboard build. It is not optional, not tabled, and not under consideration \- it is the only approach that correctly models Posterity's operational needs. No existing tool (ticketing platforms, support software, operations dashboards) maps to the specific combination of scheduled legacy content delivery, binary-path time pressure, customer-specific delivery windows, and cross-account operational events that Posterity manages. The decision to build the Priority System was made and locked June 2026\.

The Priority System determines the order of every task on the operational dashboard. Every active task is assigned a Rank- an integer from 1 (most urgent) to n (least urgent, where n is the total count of active tasks). Staff see tasks in Rank order. The Rank is computed by a three-stage equation chain that runs on a defined recalculation cadence throughout the business day and writes updated rank integers to the database between iterations.

## **Task Categories**

The dashboard operates with two distinct task categories:

**Delivery Tasks** \- scheduled post tasks. Exist because a customer's legacy is being executed during the Active phase. Time-bound, binary-path-driven, tied to a specific delivery window (8-hour or 24-hour per customer preference). The Urgency coefficient climbs as the window narrows, from 1.0 at the start of the window to 12.0 (8-hour track) or 3.5 (24-hour track) at the end. Delivery tasks include both message posts and video posts; the dashboard and all task counts distinguish between the two.

**Operational Tasks** \- everything that is not a scheduled post. General inquiries, voicemails, missed customer calls, Legacy Guard actions, automation failures, check-in events, refund requests, payment failures, social media authorization issues, Grace plan requests, Custom consultation requests, and all other events requiring staff response. Not time-bound in the same way as delivery tasks. Urgency defaults to 1.0 for all operational tasks; they climb the queue through their Need value and Age coefficient only.

## **The Upcoming Tasks Button**

A button visible on the Operational Dashboard at all access levels. Opens a live-updated popup showing the full queue snapshot at the current moment. The popup contains:

* Total tasks currently in the queue.

* Delivery tasks count, broken down by Standard/Feature Deliveries, and social media posts.

* Operational tasks count.

* Next 7 days \- each day listed individually with delivery task count (Delivery type shown separately).

* Next 30 days \-  total delivery task count for the month.

The popup is live-updated from the Priority System recalculation engine. The numbers always reflect the current queue state at the moment the button is pressed.

## **How the System Works** 

Every task enters the system with a predetermined Need value assigned by Posterity's team in the Master Weight Table. Need is subjective, static, and never recomputed at runtime \- Stage 1 (the loop-off) seals it against recursion by multiplying it by a structural constant (T\_S \= 1), which is a deliberate architectural choice that prevents the system from entering an infinite loop.

In Stage 2, Need is multiplied by Urgency \- the time-pressure coefficient read from the appropriate binary path table at the task's current binary tick position (T\_B). For delivery tasks, this coefficient climbs as the delivery window narrows. For operational tasks it is 1.0. The product is Acceleration \- how fast this task is climbing the queue right now.

In Stage 3, each task's Acceleration is scaled by a Patience Coefficient (a(T\_A)) \- a system-wide age multiplier that grows as the task ages in the queue, read from the Age Coefficient Table at the task's current Age Tick position (T\_A). This produces a per-task Comparison Value. All active tasks' Comparison Values are then sorted and ranked against each other to produce each task's final 1-to-n Rank. Stage 3 is the only cross-task operation in the system.

The system is deterministic and transparent. Every value is either a predetermined number from the Master Weight Table or a configured coefficient from a published table. There is no machine learning and no opaque scoring. Any staff member, customer, or investor can read the math and verify the result.

* Any automation failure triggers immediate Operational Dashboard alert: what failed, which customer, amount, timestamp, retry status.

* Stripe fees: \~2.9% \+ $0.30 per transaction \- not material at early scale.

* Egress bandwidth (the term for data transferred out of Supabase's servers — for example, when a recipient views or downloads a stored video file) is billed separately from storage, at $0.09/GB — over four times storage's own rate. This is currently a nonissue at Posterity's near-term scale : using the illustrative 75-customer mix, even at an average of 2–3 views per delivered video, total egress lands around 18–19GB for that cohort's first year of delivery, comfortably inside the 250GB already included in the $25/month Pro plan. Exposure is further bounded structurally: each delivery link is issued to a specific named recipient rather than shared publicly, and expires after 90 days, so there's no scenario where a single link accumulates unbounded views over time.

Download versus streaming affects this further. A download and an in-browser playback cost the same per transfer — there's no separate download fee — but a recipient who streams re-fetches the file from Supabase on every rewatch, while one who downloads generates that cost once and can revisit their local copy indefinitely afterward. Since this content is built to be revisited, encouraging download at the delivery page converts repeat viewing from a recurring cost into a one-time one. Egress isn't a live concern today, and this structural choice is what keeps it that way as volume grows, rather than something requiring active management later.

# **INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY**

The following Intellectual Property Portfolio organizes every proprietary asset within Posterity by the instrument that protects it, across five categories: trademark, copyright, patent, trade secret, and inbound and outbound rights. The distinction between those instruments is the point of the document — the same asset is often protectable by one and not by another, and treating intellectual property as a single undifferentiated category leads to filing the wrong thing at the wrong cost. Trademark and copyright items are immediate and low-cost. Patent items are speculative and expensive, and are listed for eligibility assessment rather than as a filing plan. Trade secret requires no application at all, only handling procedure. Inbound and outbound rights are not assets — they are permissions Posterity must secure from others or grant to them before commercial launch.

Two findings drive the near-term work. Trademark is the only immediate action, and it is gated: a preliminary search of the United States Patent and Trademark Office database indicates the Posterity name currently falls within the bounds of a valid trademark, and the scope of that registration must be resolved before any filing work is commissioned. Separately, because all Posterity code is generated by artificial intelligence, the codebase may not be copyrightable at all — United States copyright protects only works of human authorship, and prompting alone does not establish it. Whether protection can attach through architectural direction, selection, and revision, or whether the code must instead be protected as a trade secret, is unresolved and consequential.

# **LEGAL & COMPLIANCE**

### **Initiation**

Initiation is the first non-storage payment within the system, and the point at which Posterity's obligations to a customer begin. Payment does not commit Posterity to deliver; it commits Posterity to keep. Content is held in custody, maintained against a delivery date that has not yet been set, until the account activates and the obligation to deliver vests. 

* Payment for a plan commits Posterity to retaining and storing all content within each funded plan. This obligation attaches at the moment of payment.  
* It is a duty of retention and custody only — the obligation to deliver has not yet vested.  
* Retention continues for as long as the account's storage subscription remains current.  
* An account that lapses through non-payment and proceeds through the published notification sequence to deletion carries no continuing obligation of any kind from the point of deletion forward, and no delivery obligation ever vests.

### **Delivery**

Posterity's obligation to the customer is arrival, not route. A piece of content has been delivered when it reaches the person it was written for, by whatever method carried it there. If a preferred method cannot be completed as intended, Posterity pursues every remaining path to that same recipient by reattempting transmission and routing through alternative configured channels. The Master Recipient, whose creation is encouraged during profile setup, is a last resort for delivery once all available routes to the intended recipient are exhausted.

* Posterity's obligation to deliver content begins when an account enters the Active Phase. This duty attaches to each piece of content individually, and extends to all content scheduled within the Period of Obligation.  
* The further into the future a post is scheduled, the more uncertainty surrounds its delivery. Therefore, Posterity's ability to assuredly deliver content cannot be reasonably guaranteed beyond a five year period extending from day one of an account's entering its Active Phase.  This five year period, termed in system as the Period of Obligation, is the set timeframe in which Posterity is contractually obligated to deliver content.  Every new account beginning in a given plan year shares the same Period of Obligation, which begins with the new year as they transition from Interlude to Active Phase.   
* Content scheduled beyond Posterity's Period of Obligation within an account is treated no differently in practice. It is scheduled, monitored, and carried through all available options for delivery within the system.    
* Each particular piece of content, whether it falls within the Period of Obligation or not, is allowed a maximum of 2 Alternative Recipients chosen in descending order of priority during its creation.  The Master Recipient, a point of contact Posterity encourages customers to add during profile setup, works as the option of last resort once all available avenues for delivery are exhausted. Posterity cannot be held liable for customer input errors, contact details left out of date, or recipient-side receiving issues. ### **Digital Asset Law and Jurisdiction**

Access to a customer's digital accounts after death is governed in most states by the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act. Its hierarchy places a platform's own designation tool above all other instructions, so a Facebook Legacy Contact designation outranks both a will and Meta's terms of service. Where no platform designation exists, a will, trust, or power of attorney controls. Posterity's onboarding directs customers to set their Facebook Legacy Contact for this reason.

# **POTENTIAL ISSUES & RISK REGISTER**

All identified risks are framed as addressed \- not ongoing threats.

Business Model Risks Early adopter activation timing: customers may sign up years before accounts activate, creating a gap between revenue and service delivery. Mitigated by storage fees providing continuous revenue and the Legacy customer cap ensuring the business cannot fail financially from early-stage workload.

Financial & Legal Risks Regional escrow ordinances: holding customer funds pre-delivery could trigger escrow regulations in some jurisdictions. Addressed via Mercury Bank two-account workaround achieving same functional result as formal escrow. 

Technical Risks Meta API instability: Meta has changed API limits substantially and without warning, and access could be restricted or lost. Addressed structurally rather than by credentials. Every social media delivery carries a route off the platform by construction, so content still reaches its recipient by email or text message if Meta access fails. Posterity's obligation is arrival rather than route, and the Post Manually override remains available for individual failures.

Operational Risks Legacy tier workload: one Legacy customer equals the operational load of approximately 13 Basic customers. Addressed by Legacy customer cap (10 customers, configurable from the Operational Dashboard, where it can also be switched off entirely).
